Overview

In Helfi.Space every concern becomes a ticket – whether someone reports it in the browser or sends an email to the help desk mailbox. Agents see all tickets of their projects, assign them, reply and finally set them to “Done”. Reporters receive every reply by email and can answer it directly.

Roles

RoleWhat it may do
UserReport and follow their own tickets, see tickets they participate in, reply and attach files. Internal notes are never shown to users.
AgentSee and work on the tickets of their projects – all projects by default: status, assignee, priority, type, labels, internal notes, reply templates, bulk actions, reports.
AdministratorEverything agents may do, plus Administration: projects, users, AD, SSO, security, email, appearance, import, system.

Agents can be limited to single projects, so that e.g. facilities do not see IT tickets (Agents per project). With Active Directory the role is derived from the AD groups at every sign-in (chapter 5.4). The initial setup also creates a local administrator that keeps working when AD is unreachable.

Statuses, types and priorities

StatusMeaning
OpenNew or not started yet.
In progressSomeone is working on it.
WaitingWaiting for the reporter. If they reply, the ticket reopens.
DoneFinished. The reporter gets an email. If they reply to it, the ticket reopens (unless it was rejected or closed as a duplicate).

Types: Incident (something is broken), Request (something is needed), Task (internal work), Question; for agents also Problem (common cause of several incidents) and Change (planned change), see chapter 4. Priorities: Highest, High, Medium, Low.

Every ticket has a key made of the project key and a running number, e.g. IT-42. It also appears in the subject of all emails, e.g. [IT-42].

The interface

  • Header: logo, My work, All tickets, the Create button, search (ticket key or keyword) and, on the right, the user menu with profile, language (Deutsch/English), theme and sign-out.
  • Sidebar (agents): current project, summary, queues with live counts, board, service requests, incidents, problems, changes, my approvals, change calendar, service catalog, devices & services, knowledge, reports, reply templates and, for admins, settings.
  • Sidebar (users): My requests, depending on the setup Request a service and My approvals, and Help & knowledge.

The interface is available in German and English. Without a choice of your own it follows the browser language.

Dark mode

Under Theme in the user menu you choose how Helfi.Space looks:

  • System (default): follows the setting of Windows, macOS or the phone – dark in the evening when the device switches.
  • Light or Dark: always like that, whatever the device is set to.

The choice is stored with your account, so it also applies on your phone and on other PCs. On phones it is under Me → Theme. Emails and uploaded logos stay light so they look the same everywhere.

For everyone: signing in and making requests all

Signing in

Open the address your IT gave you, e.g. https://helfi.firma.local. With Active Directory you sign in with your Windows account – as m.muster, FIRMA\m.muster or m.muster@firma.local. If single sign-on is set up, there is an additional button such as “Sign in with company account”, or you are redirected right away.

Without a company account: if “Create account” appears below the sign-in, you can create an account yourself: enter name, email address and password and open the link in the confirmation email. If you have sent requests by email before, use the same address – they then appear in the account. You sign in with the email address. “Forgot password?” sends you a link to set a new password.

When you sign in from outside the company network, Helfi.Space may additionally ask for a 6-digit code from your authenticator app, depending on the settings (see 2.8).

Automatic sign-out: whoever does nothing for a while is signed out for security reasons. Two minutes before, Helfi.Space asks “Still there?” – Keep working simply continues. As long as you click or type, you stay signed in.

Reporting a problem

There are two ways:

  • By email to the help desk mailbox, e.g. helpdesk@firma.local. The subject becomes the summary, the text the description, attachments are included. People in To or CC automatically become participants. You receive a confirmation with the ticket key.
  • In the browser via Create (if your IT allows it): enter summary, description, type and priority, optionally attach files.

Paste screenshots: an image on the clipboard (e.g. taken with Win+Shift+S) can be pasted with Ctrl+V directly into the description or a comment.

My requests

My requests lists all tickets you reported or participate in. Use the filters at the top to narrow them down by status or “I am a participant”.

Replies and files

Open a ticket and type into the comment field at the bottom; Save or Ctrl+Enter sends your reply to IT. Alternatively, simply reply to an email from Helfi.Space – the reply lands in the right ticket automatically and quoted history is cut off.

Attach files under Attachments → Attach file. Images are shown directly, all other files are downloaded.

Request participants

Besides the reporter, further people can take part in a ticket, e.g. colleagues with the same problem. Under Request participants → Add you add colleagues who have an account (up to 20 per ticket): type a name and the suggestions appear below; pick with a click or ↑/↓ and Enter, Esc cancels.

  • Participants see the ticket, can comment and attach files and receive the same emails as the reporter.
  • They do not see internal notes and cannot change status, assignee or title. Of the reporter they only see the name, not the email address and logon name.
  • Participants can remove themselves; the reporter and agents can remove them too.
  • Agents can also enter any email address and find people directly in Active Directory (marked “AD”), by email address too. The person is taken over with name and email address from AD – they do not need to have signed in before.

Emojis

  • 🙂 button in the toolbar of the comment field (in other text fields at the bottom right): picker with search (e.g. “thumbs” or “printer”), groups and “Recently used”.
  • Short names: :thu suggests 👍 (arrow keys and Enter or Tab to accept, Esc to close). :tada: turns into 🎉 right away.
  • Classic smileys are converted as soon as a space follows: :) → 🙂, ;) → 😉, :D → 😄, :( → 🙁, :P → 😛, :O → 😮, <3 → ❤️, (y) → 👍. Ctrl+Z undoes the conversion right away.

Profile, language and password

Click your picture at the top right → Profile to reach:

  • Name and email address – editable for local accounts (a new email address requires your current password). For AD and SSO accounts both come from the company account.
  • Language of the interface and of emails sent to you.
  • What's new (also in the menu at the top right): what changed in each version. After an update Helfi.Space shows the news once by itself. Clicking the version number at the bottom of the sidebar also leads there, “Helfi.Space” next to it opens the website helfi.space.
  • Two-factor authentication setup and management.
  • Change password (local accounts only; change AD passwords in Windows as usual).

Setting up two-factor authentication

When your IT requires it (or voluntarily): Profile → Two-factor authentication → Set up now.

  1. Open an authenticator app on your phone (e.g. Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator, Aegis, 2FAS).
  2. Scan the QR code.
  3. Enter the 6-digit code shown and click Activate.
  4. Keep the 10 recovery codes safe (print them or store them in a password manager). Each works exactly once if your phone is not at hand.

For security reasons, first-time setup normally only works inside the company network (except after an SSO sign-in whose second factor was checked by the identity provider). This way nobody with a stolen password can register their own second factor from outside. Lost your phone? Sign in with a recovery code or ask IT to reset your 2FA.

Requesting a service

For frequent requests there are ready-made forms, e.g. “New employee” or “Software request”: Request a service in the sidebar. Clicking a tile opens the form; required fields are marked with *. After sending you land in the new ticket.

If an offer says “requires approval”, someone has to agree first, e.g. your manager. You can see the status at the top of the ticket.

Approving

Approvers get an email and see My approvals in the sidebar. In the ticket one click on Approve or Reject is enough, optionally with a comment. The decision is sent to the reporter as a reply; a rejection closes the request. Approvers without the agent role only see the requests they decide on, and only the reporter’s name.

Help & knowledge

Under Help & knowledge you find guides from IT, e.g. “Set up VPN when working from home”. While you report a problem, Helfi.Space already suggests matching articles (“Maybe this helps already”) as soon as you type the summary. A click opens the article without losing your report.

Was the article helpful? Click 👍 or 👎 below the article – a second click withdraws the vote. After 👎 you can briefly say what was missing or unclear; only the IT team reads it.

Below each article you can write a comment if something is missing or out of date – IT gets notified. Print / PDF prints the article or saves it as a PDF.

Rating

When your ticket is done, IT may ask you for a short rating. The “Resolved” email contains five links from “very satisfied” to “very dissatisfied”: one click is enough, no sign-in needed. On the page you can still change the stars and optionally write a comment. When signed in, you can also rate directly in the ticket.

On a smartphone

On a phone Helfi.Space opens automatically in its own interface that works like an app. At the bottom there is a bar with Requests (agents: My work and Tickets), the round ＋, Knowledge and Me. Pages slide in from the right; go back with the arrow at the top left, a swipe from the left edge or the back key. Pull lists down to refresh them.

  • Report a problem: ＋ → Report a problem. Enter a title and description, use Take photo to open the camera or File to attach pictures and documents. While you type the title, Helfi.Space shows matching help articles – maybe the solution is already there. Submit creates the request and opens it. What you have not sent yet is kept as long as the tab is open.
  • Request a service: under ＋ the offerings of the service catalog appear as tiles. Required fields are marked with *; if an offering needs approval, the form says so.
  • Ticket: the comments look like a chat. Write your reply at the bottom, attach photos or files with ＋. Agents add status changes and assignments with All next to “Activity”; the phone remembers the choice. With @ in the reply bar agents mention colleagues; under Tickets the Mentions chip shows the tickets they were mentioned in. Change title and description of your own request with Edit next to "Description".
  • Knowledge: search, categories, Recently viewed, Most read and the page tree. Long articles have a Contents list at the top, wide tables scroll sideways. Below each article you can write a comment.
  • Me: name, email address and password (local accounts), language, theme (light/dark), two-factor authentication, Install app, what's new and – only in the phone browser – Desktop view. The user menu of the desktop view (Phone view) brings you back.
  • Original email: if a request or reply came by email, it says “Text version of the email. Show original email” below it. This opens the email as it was written in a window from the bottom – with Formatted/Text to switch. Swipe down, ✕ or back closes it.

Images – attached photos and pictures in the description, replies or knowledge articles – also open in a window inside the app, not on a new page. A tap zooms to the original size (to pan around), Download saves the file; back, ✕ or swiping down closes it.

Setting up two-factor authentication on the phone: a phone cannot scan a QR code on its own screen. So under Me → Two-factor authentication → Set up now tap Open in authenticator app – the app takes over the account directly. If that does not work, copy the key shown and paste it in the app under "Enter a setup key". The QR code is still there for a second device. Then enter the 6-digit code and copy, share or save the recovery codes. If 2FA is required for your account, this works the same way right during sign-in.

Install as an app: iPhone/iPad: open Helfi.Space in Safari, tap Share at the bottom, Add to Home Screen → Add. Android: in Chrome tap ⋮ at the top right → Install app (or Me → Install app → Install now). Helfi.Space then starts full screen with its own icon and updates itself; after an update "New version – Reload" appears briefly. Without a connection the app shows a notice with Try again. Installed, Helfi.Space always stays in the phone view – links to desktop pages lead back into the app too, because there is no address bar and no back button there.

Privacy: photos are uploaded without the location where they were taken, and large photos are downscaled on the phone already. Unsent drafts are deleted when you sign out. Tickets, attachments and replies are not stored on the device, only the program files. Nothing is loaded from the internet.

For agents agents

Queues and summary

For the selected project, the sidebar shows fixed queues with live counts: All open tickets, Assigned to me, Unassigned, Waiting for reporter, Recently resolved (7 days) and All tickets. Below them Service requests and Incidents (open requests and incidents) and Mentions me (see Mentioning colleagues). Switch the project at the top of the sidebar.

The summary shows key figures, distributions by status, type and priority and the most urgent open tickets.

My work in the header collects everything assigned to you, everything unassigned and everything you reported yourself.

Board

The board shows the tickets in four columns by status. Drag a card into another column to change its status, or within a column to set the order. At the top you can filter by text, assignee (avatars), type or “Only mine”.

Ticket list and filters

All tickets shows a sortable list. Filters for project, status, type, priority, assignee and – with a project selected – component can be combined; clicking a column header sorts. The page address contains the filters – bookmark or share it.

Pages: long lists (search results too) are shown page by page. Above the table you see e.g. “Tickets 101–200 of 7956”, below it you turn pages with « ‹ 1 … 5 6 7 … 80 › ». Next to it you choose 50, 100 (default), 250 or 500 tickets per page – the browser remembers the choice. The page is part of the address (?page=3), so the back button and bookmarks work; another filter, a new search or sorting starts on page 1 again. Select all in the multiple selection takes the tickets of the current page. A search with more than 5000 matches shows the best 5000 – narrow it down then. On phones the list loads the next page while scrolling, with no upper limit.

Search

The search box at the top searches the full text of all tickets: title, description, replies, labels and attachment names. Agents also find internal notes and the people involved (also by email address and logon name). Users without the agent role find tickets by title, description, public replies, attachment names, labels and the names visible in the ticket – not by email addresses or logon names.

  • Results while typing: from two characters up to eight tickets appear with key, title, status and where they matched. Open a ticket directly with ↑/↓ and Enter or a click. Enter without a choice or Show all results opens the ticket list with all results. The empty field shows the recently opened tickets. On phones the Tickets tab works the same way.
  • Word parts match: “printer” also finds “networkprinter”.
  • Several words must all appear, in any order. "exact phrase" in quotes, -word excludes.
  • Results are sorted by relevance, title matches first. Each result shows where it matched with the words highlighted.
  • A ticket number jumps straight to the ticket.

Saved filters and custom queues

Set filters, search and sorting in the ticket list and choose Save filter. The filter appears on the left under Saved filters with its current count; the list then carries its name.

  • Personal filters are visible to you only (up to 50).
  • Admin Share with all agents: tick it when saving – the filter appears as a queue for every agent. “Assigned to me” then refers to each person, so a “My urgent ones” queue fits everyone.
  • Managing: the icon next to Saved filters opens the list to rename, sort (↑/↓) and delete. Only admins change shared queues.
  • On phones the filters are chips with a count after the queues in the Tickets tab.

Working on a ticket

  • Status via the button at the top right (e.g. “Open” → “In progress”).
  • Change assignee, priority, type, component, labels, due date and project directly in the details. “Assign to me” takes one click or the i key.
  • Click title and description to edit them.
  • Changing the project: the ticket gets a new key in the new project; the old one stays in the history. Replies to old emails are still assigned correctly.
  • Activity: tabs Comments, History (every change with person and time) and All. For a changed description, Show change shows the differences and the previous version.
  • Copy link via the chain icon at the top right.

Resolving with a closing comment

When you choose Done in the ticket's status menu, a window opens for a closing comment – write the comment and close the ticket in one step:

  • Reply to reporter (default): goes to the reporter and participants. There is then no extra “Resolved” mail – your reply already informs. Reply templates and placeholders like {reporter} work.
  • Internal note: only visible to agents; the reporter gets the normal “Resolved” mail.
  • Clicking Resolve without text simply closes the ticket. Ctrl+Enter in the text field resolves right away, Cancel leaves everything as it was.

Several tickets are resolved via the multiple selection in the ticket list (Resolve), on phones via Status → Done – both also with a reply or an internal note.

Rejecting and duplicates

Not every ticket ends with a solution. Besides Done there are therefore two more ways to close – both in the ticket's status menu (on phones under Status):

  • Reject … – the request is deliberately not carried out. The reason is required and goes to the reporter and participants as a reply; reply templates and placeholders like {reporter} work. No extra “Resolved” mail and no satisfaction survey is sent.
  • Close as duplicate … – the request is already handled in another ticket. Choose the original ticket; both are linked, and the reporter gets a short note with the original's number (or your own text). With Add reporter and participants to the original they keep following the original – only if it contains nothing confidential.

The ticket then shows Rejected or Duplicate instead of Done – in lists, under My requests and on phones –, duplicates with a link to the original. In the ticket list you filter by Resolution once the status Done is chosen; with the multiple selection you reject several tickets at once. A reply mail to it does not reopen the ticket but becomes an internal note – reopen it yourself if needed. If the reporter writes in the web interface, it reopens as usual. In the reports, rejected tickets and duplicates are counted separately and not towards the avg. resolution time.

Entering tickets for others

When a request comes in by phone, in passing or to another mailbox, enter the ticket right away on behalf of that person. It then appears under their My requests – so they get used to the ticket system.

  1. Open Create. The reporter is shown at the top, “Me” by default.
  2. Click Enter for someone else and type a name, user name or email address. From 3 characters, people from AD appear as well (marked “AD”). An email address without an account is created as an email contact.
  3. Notify the reporter by email is ticked: the person gets an email with the ticket key and link – with an account also a hint that they can report new requests themselves next time.
  4. Paste the text from the email or note and click Create.

The history shows who created the ticket for whom. Back to me undoes the choice.

Change later: in the details under Reporter click Change reporter, pick the person and confirm. The previous reporter keeps access as a request participant (except agents and pure email contacts). If a former reporter later replies to an old email, it only becomes an internal note. The same applies to mails from agent addresses matched only via the ticket number in the subject – reply to the reporter in the ticket. On phones: All details → Reporter.

Formatting replies

Above the comment field there is a toolbar – in tickets, in comments on knowledge articles and on phones:

  • AI (if set up): suggest a reply, polish, shorten, translate.
  • Formatting: bold (Ctrl+B), italic (Ctrl+I), under … underline, strikethrough and code; bullets, numbered list, quote and code block. You see the formatting right away while writing. Shortcuts work too: “- ” or “1. ” at the start of a line starts a list, “> ” a quote.
  • Link (Ctrl+K; also ticket numbers such as IT-42 or KB-3), image (or Ctrl+V), emoji (also “:)” or “:thumbsup:”) and @ to mention someone.
  • On the right: Canned responses to insert – Save as template is in the same menu.
  • Ctrl+Enter saves.

Reporters and participants get the reply formatted by email (with a readable text part for programs without HTML). On phones the bar appears above the field as soon as you write and can be swiped sideways.

Replies and internal notes

Above the comment field choose between Reply and Internal note:

  • Replies are emailed to the reporter and all participants, each in their own language.
  • Internal notes (yellow background) are only visible to agents. They are never sent by email, and pasted images stay internal as well.

Emails coming from an agent’s address never count as agent replies (sender addresses can be forged). Agents therefore always reply via the web interface. Email replies to assignment, SLA or approval mails sent to agents become internal notes and do not reopen the ticket.

Mentioning colleagues (@)

When you need someone in a ticket, mention them: type @ and a few letters in the reply field, then pick the person with a click, ↑/↓ and Enter or Tab; Esc closes the list. The name is highlighted in the comment, your own mentions in colour.

  • Who you can mention: in an internal note only agents. In a reply also the ticket's reporter, participants and approvers. People who cannot see the ticket are not in the list.
  • What happens: the person mentioned gets an email from the ticket system with your name, the comment and the link. Reporters and participants who get the reply by email anyway do not get a second one.
  • Mentions me: the sidebar lists all tickets in which you were mentioned; the number counts those you have not opened yet. In lists an @ marks new mentions, in the ticket the comment in question. Opening the ticket marks the mention as seen.
  • A mention does not make anyone a participant. To keep someone informed, add them under Request participants.

HTML emails

When a ticket or a reply arrives as an HTML email, Helfi.Space shows it formatted – with tables, bold text, colours, signature and embedded images. The Formatted | Text switch changes to the text version; the browser remembers the choice.

  • Images from the internet (often tracking pixels) are never loaded for privacy reasons; a notice states how many there were.
  • In replies the quoted history is collapsed (“Show quoted text”).
  • Editing always uses the text version. After a description has been edited, only the text applies.

Reply templates

For replies that are always the same: click Template in the comment field, type to search, insert with Enter or a click. The text lands at the cursor position and can still be adjusted before saving.

  • Placeholders are replaced on insertion: {reporter}, {ticket}, {title}, {agent}.
  • New template: write the text in the comment field and choose Save as template. Personal templates are managed in your Profile; templates for everyone are maintained by admins under Administration → Reply templates.
  • Take over examples admin: under Administration → Reply templates, Examples creates ten frequent replies for everyone – from “Confirm receipt” to “Solved – please confirm” –, in German or English and with placeholders. Adapt them as you like afterwards.

AI assistance

If an admin has set up AI assistance, agents get these helpers – depending on which functions are switched on. Everything the AI returns is a suggestion: nothing is sent or saved automatically, and requesters see none of it. The only exception, if explicitly set: assigning the component automatically.

  • AI summary below the description: Summarize creates an overview “What it is about – What happened so far – Open / next step”. It is kept; if the ticket changes, a note says so and Create again fetches a current one.
  • AI in the reply field (the sparkle icon on phones): Suggest a reply drafts an answer from the ticket history. If you have already typed notes, the AI builds the reply from them. With text in the field there are also Polish text, Shorten and Translate (German/English).
  • The suggestion replaces the text in the field; Undo brings back the previous text. Helfi adds the salutation and closing itself when sending the mail – please read and adjust the suggestion before saving.

Detecting the component

If Detect component is on, the AI looks at new tickets that no component rule matches and works out which component fits. In Suggest mode the details show “AI suggests: …” with a short reason: Accept sets the component (and gives the ticket to the responsible person if nobody is assigned yet), Dismiss hides the suggestion. On phones the suggestion is under All details. If you pick a different component yourself instead, that counts as a correction. In Assign automatically mode the AI sets the component directly; the history says “by AI”.

Detecting duplicates

If Detect duplicates is on, Helfi.Space checks every new ticket: first it searches similar open tickets of the same project from the last 30 days without AI; only if there are any does it ask the AI whether the new ticket reports the same. The ticket then shows “Possible duplicate of IT-12” with a reason. Close as duplicate opens the dialog with the original filled in (see Rejecting and duplicates), Not a duplicate hides the hint. The AI never closes anything itself; reporters do not see the hint.

Writing knowledge articles

In the article editor AI is the first button of the toolbar. It works on the selection – without one on the whole article – and keeps the formatting: Polish text, Shorten, As a step-by-step guide (notes become numbered steps), Translate into German/English and Suggest title and category (to accept or adjust). For a new article from a ticket there is also Write a draft from IT-42: problem – cause – solution from the ticket history, without names, addresses, computer names and ticket numbers. Undo brings back the previous text; please check the draft before publishing.

Several tickets at once

In All tickets select tickets with the check boxes (Shift+click for a range, x for the highlighted ticket). The action bar offers:

  • Resolve and Status – each with an optional comment: as Reply to reporters (mail to reporter and participants) or as Internal note, also with a template and placeholders per ticket.
  • Comment – reply or internal note for all selected tickets without changing the status.
  • Link … – connect all selected tickets with one ticket, e.g. “is caused by IT-42”; optionally with status and comment right away (“the cause is being fixed in IT-42”).
  • Assign, Priority and Move to another project.
  • Set or remove the component – once all selected tickets belong to one project. Nobody is assigned automatically; whoever is responsible sees the tickets through their filters.

Reports

Reports shows, for a period (7, 30, 90 days, this year or custom) and optionally one project: created, resolved and open tickets, “Resolved per agent”, the “Created and resolved” trend and a table per agent (resolved, open assigned, worked on, replies, internal notes, average resolution time). The table can be sorted and downloaded as CSV. Once components are used, By component shows the tickets of the period per component, how many are open, rejected or duplicates, and the average resolution time (without rejected ones and duplicates).

Tickets are never deleted

Tickets are records: who reported what and when, and what was done? That is why they cannot be deleted, not even by administrators. Finished work is set to “Done”. Against spam, use a sender rule “Ignore” (5.7) – then no ticket is created in the first place.

IT service management (ITIL) agents

Helfi.Space covers the most important ITIL processes without forcing you to use all of them: incidents and major incidents, service levels, problems, changes, knowledge, service catalog, devices and services, and satisfaction. Whatever is not switched on or set up stays invisible.

Links and subtasks

In the Links section of a ticket – faster via the Link button at the top next to the status, on phones under Details – you connect tickets. Search the target ticket by number or keyword; while the field is empty, Helfi suggests possible causes (open problems, major incidents) and similar tickets. Kinds: “relates to”, “duplicates” (optionally resolving the duplicate right away), “blocks”, “causes” and “is subtask of”. Subtask directly creates a task with an assignee; a bar shows how many are done.

Impact, urgency and major incident

In the details you set impact (how many are affected?) and urgency (how fast must it be fixed?). When both are set, the priority follows the ITIL matrix, e.g. high/high = Highest.

Mark a severe incident affecting many people as a major incident. All agents then see a red bar at the top. Link individual reports with “causes”. Inform everyone affected sends a message to the reporters and participants of all open linked tickets; with “resolve” they are resolved together.

Service levels (SLA)

With SLAs switched on, every ticket from users has two clocks: first response (until IT’s first public reply) and resolution. Both only run during business hours; holidays and, optionally, the status “Waiting” pause them. At 80 % of the time the ticket turns yellow and the assignee (otherwise the project lead) gets an email; when the target is exceeded it turns red. The queue SLA at risk shows all tickets running short.

admin Targets per priority, business hours, holidays and warning threshold under Administration → Service level. Default: off.

Problems and known errors

When the same incidents pile up, there is usually a common cause. Create problem in the ticket creates a problem and links the incident. Attach further incidents with “causes”. In the problem you record the root cause and workaround. Marked as a known error, agents see the workaround in all linked incidents and can send it as a reply with one click. The tab Known errors under Knowledge lists them all.

Change management

Planned changes to IT (firewall rule, update, move) are tickets of type Change. There are three kinds:

  • Normal: needs approval.
  • Standard: pre-approved routine based on a template.
  • Emergency: implement immediately, approve afterwards.

The Change section guides you through the phases Draft → Awaiting approval → Approved → Scheduled → Implementing → Review → Closed. Helfi.Space names missing details (reason, time window, implementation and backout plan) at the respective step. Approvers decide in the ticket or under My approvals. When closing you choose the outcome; for “With issues” and “Rolled back” a review is required.

The change calendar shows all planned changes in the month; overlaps are marked red.

admin Approvers, rule (“one approval is enough” or “everyone must approve”) and templates for standard changes under Administration → Change.

Knowledge base

Knowledge collects guides and solutions. Articles are internal (agents only) or public (everyone signed in, also suggested while reporting). Search ranks title matches higher. From a solved ticket, Knowledge article at the top of the ticket creates a draft; remove personal details before publishing. From three headings on, the article view shows a table of contents on the right.

Example articles: if the knowledge base is still empty, Examples takes over ready sample articles – such as “How to report a problem well”, “Setting up VPN” or “Changing or forgetting your password”. They show how a good article is built and are created internal: replace the details in square brackets with your own and then make them public.

Rating and feedback

Below every article there is “Was this article helpful?” with 👍 and 👎. The article list shows the rating per article; the Needs rework filter lists articles with 👎 votes, most first. On the article, Feedback opens the notes on what was missing. Once you have reworked the article, Reset rating sets the votes back to zero – the notes stay under “Earlier feedback”.

The toolbar stays at the top while scrolling and offers text style (normal, heading 1–3, quote, code block), bold, italic, underline, under … also strikethrough and inline code, bulleted list, numbered list, checklist, link, image, emoji, info panel (note, tip, warning), table and divider.

  • Images: with Ctrl+V from the clipboard, by drag & drop or with the image button. Clicking an image sets its size (small, medium, full width) and a caption.

HTTPS and reverse proxy

Helfi.Space itself speaks HTTP on port 8080. In production put a reverse proxy with HTTPS in front of it (Synology reverse proxy, Nginx Proxy Manager, Caddy, Traefik …). Then set HELFI_BASE_URL=https://helfi.firma.local – cookies are marked Secure automatically.

helfi.firma.local {
    reverse_proxy 10.0.10.20:8080
}

Enter the proxy’s address in HELFI_TRUST_PROXY, e.g. 10.0.10.5 (several separated by commas: loopback, 10.0.10.5). Without it Helfi.Space only trusts X-Forwarded-For from its own host (loopback); values such as true or a number are ignored with a warning in the log. If a proxy listed by its address (not a range) forwards a request without X-Forwarded-For, it counts as from outside. If the proxy runs on another host, Helfi.Space then only sees the proxy: the brake against password guessing then hits everyone together, and every request counts as “from outside” to be safe.

Redirect http:// to https://

Many proxies – including the Synology reverse proxy – cannot redirect custom addresses to HTTPS themselves; whoever types http://… otherwise lands on the Synology's start page. Helfi.Space does it: as soon as the https:// address from HELFI_BASE_URL has been opened once over HTTPS through the configured proxy, requests the proxy forwards over HTTP (X-Forwarded-Proto: http) are redirected there permanently. A proxy without any HTTPS is therefore never redirected into nothing; HELFI_FORCE_HTTPS=1 redirects right away, 0 never. The target never comes from the request. Add a second rule for this:

  1. Control Panel → Login Portal → Advanced → Reverse Proxy → Create.
  2. Source: protocol HTTP, hostname as in the HTTPS rule, port 80.
  3. Destination: exactly as in the HTTPS rule (HTTP, host, port 8080). The Synology sets the X-Forwarded-Proto header itself.
  4. Check Let's Encrypt: port 80 stays open on the router. Afterwards renew the certificate once under Control Panel → Security → Certificate. If that fails, delete the HTTP rule again – the existing certificate stays valid.

HSTS (one year, this address only) is sent by Helfi.Space only where it had to redirect itself – as behind the Synology. A proxy that redirects on its own (Nginx Proxy Manager with “Force SSL”) keeps its own HSTS header, so there are no duplicate headers, which checkers such as the Mozilla HTTP Observatory count as an error. HELFI_HSTS=off switches HSTS off entirely. HELFI_HSTS=preload adds preload (only if all subdomains speak HTTPS for good).

Initial setup

On the first visit to HELFI_BASE_URL the setup wizard starts. It asks for the setup code from the container log (docker compose logs helfi) and creates the local administrator and the first project. It then continues straight to the AD setup.

Active Directory

Administration → Active Directory

  1. Enter the domain controller with the name in its certificate (e.g. dc01.firma.local). Encryption: LDAPS (636) or StartTLS (389).
  2. Fetch certificate, compare the SHA-256 fingerprint with the one on the server, then Accept certificate.
    Windows: certlm.msc → Personal → certificate of the DC. Samba: openssl x509 -in /var/lib/samba/private/tls/cert.pem -noout -fingerprint -sha256
  3. Enter a service account, e.g. svc-helfi@firma.local – a normal domain account without special rights.
  4. Groups for administrators and agents, e.g. Helfi-Admins and Helfi-Agents (name or DN, nested groups count). Optionally a group of “Allowed users”. In addition every project can have a project group (Agents per project).
  5. Test connection, ideally with a test sign-in – the test also checks the project groups and, with a test sign-in, names the person's projects. An empty base DN is filled in; a Microsoft certificate authority (AD CS) that is found can be accepted too, so renewed DC certificates stay valid.
  6. Enable AD sign-in and save.

Users are created at their first sign-in or when an agent adds them as a participant from AD (Suggest participants from AD, on by default, requires the service account). Their role follows the groups at every sign-in unless an admin locked it under Users with “Role locked”. Helfi.Space checks daily whether the certificate still matches and whether it expires soon, and shows problems as a notice.

Single sign-on

Administration → Single sign-on (OpenID Connect: Microsoft Entra ID, ADFS, Keycloak, Authentik …)

  1. Create an app at the provider and enter the redirect URI shown, e.g. https://helfi.firma.local/auth/oidc/callback.
  2. Enter issuer URL, client ID and client secret and click Test. Entra ID: https://login.microsoftonline.com/<tenant ID>/v2.0 and add the groups claim under Token configuration.
  3. Enter the admin and agent groups (group IDs for Entra ID).
  4. Optionally “redirect straight to SSO”. The normal sign-in remains available at /login?local=1.

SSO and AD together: with “Merge with AD accounts for domain” (e.g. firma.local), m.muster@firma.local from SSO is the same account as the AD user m.muster. Only enter it if the provider does not allow guest accounts from other organisations. If the token contains only the email address (no preferred_username/UPN), accounts are only merged if the provider reports it as verified (email_verified). Email addresses from SSO are only taken over if they are verified or equal the user name of the allowed domain.

Stable via the AD identifier (recommended): under Claim with AD identifier enter the claim holding objectSid or objectGUID – onprem_sid (optional claim) for Entra ID, a custom mapping for Keycloak/Authentik. Then accounts are merged only this way, not by freely chosen names. With a service account the AD account is checked on every SSO sign-in (disabled, group, role); if the AD is unreachable, the SSO sign-in is cancelled.

Email

Administration → Email

  • Fetch mailbox (IMAP): server, user and password of the help desk mailbox. New emails become tickets, replies become comments. Processed emails are marked as read or moved to a folder – a separate folder is recommended. Skipped emails and delivery failure reports stay in the inbox, marked as read, and are not deleted automatically. Processed emails get their own flag; if the mailbox is read in Outlook in parallel, they are still not lost (if the mail server supports custom flags, otherwise better not read it in parallel).
  • Replies by ticket key in the subject are only accepted from the reporter, request participants and everyone who has already written in the ticket; anyone who merely guesses the key gets a new ticket. Replies to mails from Helfi.Space are matched via the Message-ID.
  • Sending (SMTP): ideally use the mailbox address as sender, so replies come back into the ticket.
  • The sender is always the ticket system – never an agent’s address, no personal signatures. So the reporter knows who looks after them, reply and resolved mails end with “Kind regards”, the agent’s name and the system name; if the ticket is assigned to someone else, “Your contact person: …” is added. In the browser, users see the field as “Your contact”.
  • Look up senders in Active Directory: unknown senders are searched in AD and created as AD users right away. Otherwise they become email contacts; when they sign in later, their tickets are theirs.
  • Notifications: each can be switched off – receipt confirmation, replies and resolved to reporters and participants, assignment to agents, mail to newly added participants and to people @-mentioned.
  • Reminder to project leads: whoever does not look into the ticket system every day would otherwise miss new tickets. Enter a number under After days without assignee (0 = off); then the lead of each project gets one mail a day at the set time (optionally Monday to Friday only): all open tickets nobody has been assigned to for that many days, with a link to each ticket and to the filtered list. If a project has no lead (or the lead is not an agent of the project), the mail goes to all admins. If nothing is left lying, no mail is sent. Check and send now sends right away – handy for trying it out.
  • Recipients in To/CC become participants (can be turned off) – for new tickets and replies to mails from Helfi.Space, not when a reply is matched only by the key in the subject.
  • Retention: “Delete processed emails after … days” (default: never). Only the folder for processed emails is cleaned, never the inbox.

Mail loop protection and sender rules

  • Delivery failure reports and read receipts never become tickets and are never answered. The affected address gets no emails for 7 days (Blocked recipients, can be released early there). Only addresses from real header lines of the report or from its message/delivery-status part are blocked – and only if the report refers to a mail Helfi.Space sent to exactly that address.
  • Sender rules (address or pattern like *@pve.firma.local): “Create ticket, never reply” – optionally into a separate project – or “Ignore”.
  • Automatically generated emails (Proxmox, NAS, monitoring; recognised by Auto-Submitted, Precedence: bulk or noreply@) are only accepted with the rule “Create ticket, never reply”. Recently skipped emails lists sender and reason.
  • More than 20 new tickets per hour from the same sender are ignored.

Projects

Administration → Projects: create projects (key such as IT or NET and a name), set the lead, archive (kept with all tickets, disappears from the selection) or delete. A project with tickets can only be deleted if its tickets are moved to another project; a backup is made automatically beforehand.

Agents per project

By default every agent works on all projects – as before; nothing changes with the update. With several departments, e.g. IT and facilities, you decide who works on which tickets. An agent for selected projects only is a normal user for every other project: there they only see their own requests (and those they take part in) – without internal notes, history and agent tools.

In Projects the Agents button opens the window for it:

  • Project group (AD/SSO): e.g. IT-Helpdesk for the IT project. Members of this group become agents of this project on sign-in; whoever is in several project groups works on several projects. Whoever is also in the general agent group stays responsible for all projects. A project group never turns anyone into a plain user and never removes admin rights. Membership is checked at every sign-in; when you change the group, the old memberships stop counting at once (until the next sign-in).
  • Assigned by hand: tick agents – for accounts without AD or in addition. Agents with “All projects” need no tick.

Restricted everywhere: ticket lists and board, search (internal notes of other projects find nothing), queues and counts, summary, reports, SLA, problems, changes and major incidents, links, the ticket history of a device, AI functions and @-mentions in internal notes. Tickets can only be assigned to agents of the project and only be moved into one's own projects; a ticket moved into a project its assignee does not work on is unassigned afterwards. Shared are the knowledge base, reply templates, devices & services and the people search. Admins always work on all projects.

Example: enter the project group IT-Helpdesk for the IT project and Facilities for the HM project, and put the accounts into the matching AD groups; whoever works in both departments joins both. Take accounts out of the general agent group – otherwise they stay responsible for all projects.

Components

Administration → Components (or the Components button in Projects) splits a project into areas of responsibility, e.g. “VPN & remote access” or “Printers & scanners”. Each component has a name, a short description, a responsible person, optionally a deputy, keywords and optionally senders. Only agents see components.

  • How a ticket gets its component: a matching sender (address or pattern such as *@zabbix.firma.local) counts first. Otherwise the keywords decide: a hit in the title counts 3, in the description 1, each keyword once. The component with the most points wins, on a tie the one further up – change the order with ↑/↓. From 4 letters a keyword also counts inside a word (“print” finds “printer”), shorter ones only as a whole word (“pc” does not find “PCIe”). If nothing fits, the field stays empty.
  • Assignment: if nobody is assigned yet, the responsible person gets the ticket – if they are deactivated, the deputy. If an agent picks someone when creating the ticket, that choice stays.
  • The history says why: “component VPN & remote access (keywords: vpn, forticlient)” and “assigned to Anna Agentin (responsible for VPN & remote access)”.
  • Changing: agents pick the component when creating a ticket (“Automatic” lets the rules decide) and change it in the details; on phones under All details. Moving the ticket to another project clears it, because components belong to a project.
  • Archive or delete: archived components stay visible on old tickets but are no longer assigned. Deleting empties the field on the tickets; the tickets themselves stay.
  • Import: if the source system provides components, the first component of a ticket comes along and is created in the target project if it does not exist yet. Add the responsible person afterwards.

Taking over examples

Testing the rules

Below the list, enter a title, a description and optionally a sender. Check shows which component a new ticket would get, why, and who it would go to.

Sorting existing tickets

After the import or when components are added, many tickets have no component yet. At the bottom of the page you assign one afterwards:

  1. Preview shows how many tickets have no component, how many a rule matches and how many go to the AI. By default only open tickets count; done ones only with Also done tickets ticked. At most 1000 tickets go to the AI per run (start again for the rest); the confirmation before starting says how much text goes to the AI and points out possible costs for an AI on the internet.
  2. Start sorting applies the rules (sender, keywords) first. If the AI function Detect component is on, all remaining tickets then go to the AI one after the other – in the background, with a progress bar, can be cancelled at any time. Helfi.Space asks first, as the AI receives title and description.
  3. Review AI suggestions: by default the AI sets nothing itself. The list shows ticket, suggestion and reason; you accept or dismiss single ones or several at once. With Set directly the AI enters the component right away (“by AI” in the history).

When sorting afterwards, no mails are sent, nobody gets tickets assigned, and the “Updated” time stays – lists keep their order. Tickets the AI already checked (suggestion open, dismissed or “none fits”) are skipped by a new run; check again asks once more, e.g. after you improved the component descriptions.

Cutting components sensibly

  • By responsibility, not by technology: a component is what one person (or a small team) is in charge of. Whoever looks after printers and scanners does not need two components.
  • 6–12 rather than 40: components that are too fine get picked wrongly and clutter the reports. Labels handle finer distinctions.
  • Every component needs a responsible person, ideally with a deputy – otherwise tickets end up “Unassigned” again.
  • Keywords from real tickets: use the words your reporters actually write (“Outlook”, “FortiClient”, “password expired”), not IT jargon. Avoid words found in almost every mail (e.g. “email” or “phone” from signatures).
  • Check with the test field and after a few weeks look at Reports → By component: many tickets “Without component” mean keywords are missing.
  • Senders for systems: monitoring, backup or printers that send mails themselves are matched reliably with a sender pattern.

Import from another ticket system

Administration → Import takes over tickets with comments and attachments from Zammad, Znuny / OTRS, GLPI, Zendesk, Freshdesk, Jira (cloud, Server/Data Center) or a CSV file. Credentials are not stored.

  1. Choose the source and connect:

    • Zendesk: address, email and API token; with an administrator account archived tickets come along too.
    • Freshdesk: address and API key from the profile (at most 30,000 tickets via the list).
    • Jira: address and API token (cloud) or personal access token (Server/Data Center).
    • CSV file: upload it and map the columns – recognised columns are prefilled, only the title is required. A project / queue column spreads the tickets over several projects.
  2. Choose projects (groups, queues, entities or projects) and the target project.
  3. Map statuses and types (suggestions are pre-filled).
  4. Import – with progress, can be cancelled.

Ticket numbers are kept where possible (SUP-123 stays SUP-123, Zammad #31011 becomes SD-31011; very long, date-based numbers get a new one, the old one is kept in the history). Internal notes and their files stay internal. People are matched by email address, and no emails are sent. Before every import starts, Helfi.Space creates a backup. A second run only updates what changed and never creates duplicates – so you can import once as a trial and again on the cut-over day (for CSV with the same name of the source).

Updates

With container management (recommended)

Dockhand, Watchtower or Tugtainer detect new images of ghcr.io/soleil0815/helfi.space.ticket:latest and update the container – ideally at night. Besides new versions, a security update <version>-s<n> appears once a month on patch day (the second Tuesday): same program, only newer packages, tested and checked like every release. Helfi.Space backs up the database itself when a new version starts (data/backups/helfi-vor-<version>-….sqlite) and then migrates it.

Manually

./update.sh             # latest version
./update.sh 1.5.0       # specific version (recorded in .env)
./update.sh -y          # without questions
./update.sh --cleanup   # show leftovers of earlier updates and delete them after confirmation
./update.sh --offline helfi-1.5.0-amd64.tar.gz 1.5.0   # without internet

update.sh fetches the new version, takes over a newer docker-compose.yml (the old one is kept as docker-compose.yml.alt-…), starts the container and waits for the health check. If the new version does not start, it restores the previous version together with its database backup; .env then holds the previous version again. It runs with /bin/sh, also on a Synology. --cleanup never touches data/, backups, .env or the current docker-compose.yml.

  • docker compose up fails: error message, exit code 1, .env and docker-compose.yml are restored. If there is a previous version, update.sh first asks whether to start it again (automatically with -y).
  • Still starting: if the new version is still “starting” after HELFI_UPDATE_WAIT, update.sh asks whether to wait once more (automatically once with -y).
  • Database not restored: update.sh says so explicitly, e.g. if the backups are not readable (then sudo ./update.sh -y) or there is no backup or only an incomplete one.
  • --offline without a version: the version is taken from the image and recorded in .env, otherwise update.sh aborts. A relative path is taken from the current folder.
  • HELFI_UPDATE_WAIT (time to wait, default 120 seconds) and HELFI_IMAGE_REPO are set on the command line, not in .env, e.g. HELFI_UPDATE_WAIT=300 ./update.sh. For your own registry, also change image: in docker-compose.yml; it then counts as customized and is no longer replaced automatically.

After an update the browser loads the new interface immediately (from version 1.5.0). If an older version still shows the old interface, press Ctrl+Shift+R once.

Pinning a version and going back

  1. Enter a fixed version in .env, e.g. HELFI_VERSION=1.4.0.
  2. If the newer version has already migrated the database, the older one does not start and names the matching backup in the log. Then: 
    docker compose down
cp data/helfi.sqlite data/helfi.sqlite.new
cp data/backups/helfi-vor-1.5.0-<date>.sqlite data/helfi.sqlite
rm -f data/helfi.sqlite-wal data/helfi.sqlite-shm
docker compose up -d

Backup and restore

  • Automatic: daily, before every version change, before deleting a project and before every import starts, in data/backups/. A refused project deletion creates no backup. Manual: Administration → System → Back up now.
  • Backups to keep (default 10): this many of the newest backups are kept per kind – daily, manual, before update, before project deletion and before import. Older ones are deleted when saving and from then on, at least the newest of each kind always stays. The list below shows kind, date, size and the total space used; 🗑 deletes a single backup.
  • Download: ⤓ next to a backup downloads it as a ZIP file, Back up and download creates a new one and downloads it right away. The ZIP holds the database (helfi.sqlite) and an INFO.txt with restore instructions. Sign-in sessions are removed from it so nobody can take over a signed-in session with the file – after restoring, everyone signs in again. secret.key and attachments are left out on purpose – the key would decrypt the stored passwords. A new installation therefore also needs secret.key from the Docker host. Every download is logged.
  • Packed: backups are stored as helfi-….sqlite.gz in data/backups/ and need only a fraction of the space. Each packed file is unpacked and compared with the original before it is stored. The backup before a version change stays unpacked for a day, because update.sh restores exactly this one if the new version does not start; afterwards it is packed too. Older unpacked backups are packed once in the background after startup. Restoring by hand: gunzip -c data/backups/helfi-….sqlite.gz > data/helfi.sqlite with the container stopped.
  • Each backup is first written as a .part file and only renamed once complete. A .part file left behind after a crash is never used and is removed by the next clean-up after one hour.
  • The backups contain only the database. Always back up the whole data/ folder to another drive – including attachments, logo and secret.key.
  • Without secret.key (or the HELFI_SECRET you set) stored passwords and 2FA keys can no longer be decrypted.

Locked out? (emergency access)

Try the normal ways first:

SituationWay out
AD unreachableSign in with the local emergency admin from the initial setup.
SSO redirects right away and failshttps://…/login?local=1 opens the normal sign-in page.
Phone with the authenticator lostSign in with a recovery code, or another admin resets 2FA under Users.
Password forgottenForgot password on the sign-in page – for admins only if Security → Registration and forgotten password is set to “all local accounts” and mail goes out.
AD admin lost the admin groupThe emergency admin or another admin gives the role back under Users.
Too many failed attemptsWait 15 minutes or docker compose restart helfi.

If none of that helps, there is the emergency command on the Docker host – in the folder with docker-compose.yml:

docker compose exec helfi helfi-admin list             # show local admin accounts
docker compose exec helfi helfi-admin reset admin      # new password, 2FA off, sessions ended
docker compose exec helfi helfi-admin create notfall   # create a new local admin
  • reset asks for the new password twice without echo (at least 10 characters). --generate creates a random password and shows it once; --keep-2fa keeps two-factor sign-in set up. A deactivated account becomes active again. Then sign in at /login?local=1 and set up 2FA again.
  • create adds a local admin (--name "First Last", --email address), e.g. if the emergency admin was deleted.
  • Without a terminal (script, scheduler): printf '%s\n' "$PW" | docker compose exec -T helfi helfi-admin reset admin --password-stdin.
  • The command only works on local accounts; AD and SSO accounts are managed there.

The command is only reachable with a shell on the Docker host – whoever has that can read the data folder anyway. It does not work over the network, and it runs as PUID/PGID, never as root. Every use is listed under Administration → System → Emergency access and emailed to all admins with an email address.

Security

  • Passwords of local accounts are stored with scrypt, stored credentials (service account, mailbox, SSO) are encrypted with AES-256-GCM and never sent to the browser.
  • Anyone who changes server, port, encryption, service account or user, issuer or client ID for AD, IMAP, SMTP or SSO, weakens the certificate check (including “Fetched certificate” → “System certificate authorities”) or stores a different AD certificate, must enter the password or secret again in the same step, otherwise nothing is saved. This way a stored secret never goes to a different server; connection tests with a weakened check do not send it either.
  • Sessions end after the time set under Automatic sign-out without use (without a setting HELFI_SESSION_HOURS, default 12 hours; administrators 60 minutes) and at the latest 30 days after sign-in. Disabled accounts get the same message at sign-in as for a wrong password.
  • Two-factor authentication per RFC 6238, each code works once, recovery codes are stored only as hashes.
  • Brake on wrong passwords, cookies HttpOnly/SameSite, CSRF protection, strict Content Security Policy. The interface loads nothing from the internet.
  • HTML emails are cleaned and shown in an isolated frame without scripts.
  • The container runs without root rights, with a read-only program part, without additional Linux capabilities and with no-new-privileges.
  • Tickets are never deleted; every change is recorded in the history.
